I

The Concept

Financial doom

“In the long run we are all dead.”

John Maynard Keynes, A Tract on Monetary Reform, 1923

Deflation is a requiem for money. Every song is a slow funeral for a currency that was promised to hold its value and quietly didn’t: debt rolled into more debt, money printed to cover the printing, and savings melting into the price of bread.

The band takes its aim at the Keynesian creed of spend now, borrow forever, and let the long run take care of itself. Deflation plays the long run. It is the hangover after the stimulus, the bill that finally arrives, the hour when paper wealth is weighed and found to be paper.

The name is a curse and a prophecy at once. Inflation is the fever. Deflation is what is left when the fever breaks: prices falling back to earth, illusions collapsing, and the ash of a thousand paper promises drifting down over the stage.

II

The Sound

Tuned to the floor

⟁

The Scream

Harsh, black-metal-style shrieks: the voice of the debtor, the saver, the bag-holder. Raw, ragged and inhuman, howling from the top of the mix.

◯

The Chant

Low, clean, droning song set against the scream. Half dirge, half invocation, it is the voice of the ritual leader reading the ledger aloud.

▽

The Weight

Guitars and bass detuned almost to the floor, buried in fuzz and distortion. Thick, rotten tone that hangs in the air like smoke after a fire.

⧗

The Slow Bleed

Crushing, glacial tempos borrowed from sludge and funeral doom. Each riff decays the way purchasing power does: too slowly to notice until everything is gone.

III

The Rite

Stage presence

On stage, Deflation is not a band playing songs. It is a congregation performing a rite.

The members appear in long black robes, hooded and faceless, like shamans of a collapsing economy. The imagery is deliberately occult and unsettling, sigils, candles, smoke, and inverted signs of wealth, because the true religion being exorcised is the worship of easy money.

  1. ProcessionRobed figures enter through smoke as the first drone swells.
  2. InvocationThe chant calls up the spirits of every currency that died before ours.
  3. The LedgerDebts are read aloud and answered by the scream.
  4. The BurningPaper value is offered to the fire.
  5. AshSilence, embers, and the slow fall of what remains.

The order of the rite above describes the stage concept, not a fixed setlist.

IV

Burning Money

Ntawv nyiaj · the Hmong root

scroll to burn

The idea of burning money did not start with this band. It comes from home.

In Hmong tradition, families offer ntawv nyiaj, paper spirit money, to those who have passed on. At funerals, the qeej plays Hlawv Ntawv, the song that accompanies the burning of paper and incense for the deceased. Gold and silver paper is folded into small boats and ingots, and at the New Year families call the household’s souls home and send spirit money to the ancestors, who in return watch over the living.

In that ritual, burning paper is an act of love and care: wealth is sent onward so that the ancestors are provided for on their journey. It is sacred, and Deflation treats it that way.

The band’s imagery grows from that inheritance and turns it toward the present. If paper can be burned and carried to the spirit world, what is our own paper really worth? Every fiat note is already being quietly burned by inflation, its value drifting up the chimney one percent at a time. Deflation sets that burning on stage, where everyone can watch the paper turn to ash.

The occult imagery belongs to the band’s critique of debt and false wealth. It is not a description of Hmong spiritual practice, which the band honors as the ancestral rite its imagery grows from.
